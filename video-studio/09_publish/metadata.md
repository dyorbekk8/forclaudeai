## Description
What if every lie you told made you one year older?

Not your mind. Just your body. Instantly. And it never goes back.

You're fifteen. You tell your mom you finished your homework… and your spine goes pop. So you decide to become the most honest kid alive. It works — until the reflex lies start slipping out. And until Saturday, when Grandpa asks the one question you can't answer honestly.

Would you spend a year of your life on a kind lie? Tell us in the comments.

Is any of this real? The science in this episode:
- In a survey of 1,000 U.S. adults, people reported 1.65 lies a day on average; about 60% told no lies in the previous 24 hours, and about half of all lies came from roughly 5% of people. — Serota, Levine & Boster (2010), "The Prevalence of Lying in America", Human Communication Research 36(1).
- At 1.65 lies a day, a 15-year-old's body would reach 90 in about 45 days (75 ÷ 1.65). (our math)
- For many people living with dementia, hearing again that a loved one has died can feel like fresh grief; whether to always tell the truth is debated in dementia care. — Mental Health Foundation (2016), "What is truth?"
