## Description
What if every time someone told you "you can't"... you got stronger?

You're fourteen. You weigh forty-five kilos. And the second the school bully says you can't lift that bar, it goes up like paper. So you start collecting doubts. Coaches, sisters, the entire internet. Until the whole world starts believing in you instead.

Would you want to be strong only when people doubt you? Tell us in the comments.

Is any of this real?
- In 1968, Robert Rosenthal and Lenore Jacobson told teachers that certain randomly chosen pupils were about to "bloom". By the end of the year, those pupils showed larger average I.Q. gains than their classmates (strongest in the youngest grades). This became known as the Pygmalion effect; later studies found real but usually smaller effects. — Rosenthal, R. & Jacobson, L. (1968). Pygmalion in the Classroom.
