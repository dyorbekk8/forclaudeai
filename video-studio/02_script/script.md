# EP01 — "What If You Aged 1 Year Every Time You Lied?"

Target runtime ≈ 5:45 · Narrator 2nd person, present tense · 150 WPM
Why this topic: rule+cost mechanic (IFNation "sorry→inch" = 1102x outlier) +
"lie" theme (Miko "lie count" 675K on 7K subs) + family emotional anchor
(Ifverse 500 IQ formula). No long-form animated video on this exact mechanic exists.

Running device: **BODY AGE badge** top-right (like Ifverse's power bar). Every lie:
spine POP sfx + badge flips +1 with a red flash.

Cast: YOU (Eli, 15, yellow hoodie) · MOM · GRANDPA Walt (dementia) · SAM (best friend) ·
NORA (little sister, 7) · Grandma ROSIE (photo only, died 2 yrs ago)

---

## ACT 0 — COLD OPEN (kitchen, morning, warm)
- MOM: Did you finish your homework?
- YOU: Yep.
- N: Something in your spine goes... pop. Your hoodie gets tighter. Your voice drops. And there's a hair on your chin that wasn't there a second ago. You were fifteen. Now... you're sixteen.

## ACT 1 — PREMISE + SCIENCE
- N: Because in this world, every time you lie, you get one year older. Not your mind. Just your body. Instantly. And it never goes back.
- N: That sounds easy to avoid. Until you do the math. In one study, researchers asked a thousand American adults how many lies they'd told in the last twenty-four hours. The average was one point six five a day. At that rate, a fifteen-year-old would be ninety in about six weeks.
- N: But here's the weird part. Six out of ten people said they hadn't told a single lie that day. And half of all the lies came from just one person in twenty.
- N: So the rule is simple. Be one of the six.

## ACT 2 — "IT'S AMAZING" (honesty comedy)
- N: Day one. Your best friend Sam just got a haircut.
- SAM: Be honest. Is it good?
- YOU: It looks like... a decision.
- N: Your teacher asks if you read chapter four. You didn't. Detention. Your mom made meatloaf. You say you're "not that hungry." Which is true. Because you saw the meatloaf.
- N: And then something strange happens. People start trusting you. Is this text too clingy? Ask Eli. Does this shirt work? Ask Eli. By Friday, half the school wants your opinion. Because you are the only person in the building who can't lie to them.

## ACT 3 — THE SLIP (escalation)
- N: Then the lies start slipping out. Not big ones. Reflex ones.
- MOM: Did you eat the last cookie?
- YOU: No.
- N: Pop. Seventeen. Someone bumps you in the hallway. "I'm fine." Eighteen. Your aunt calls. "How are you?" "Good, thanks!" Nineteen.
- N: Nineteen years old. In tenth grade. You shave every morning. The bus driver asks for your I.D. And at breakfast, your mom just keeps staring at you.
- MOM: Honey... are you okay?
- N: And that's the trap. You can't say "I'm fine." So you say nothing at all.

## ACT 4 — GRANDPA (the anchor)
- N: On Saturdays, you visit Grandpa Walt. He taught you to ride a bike. He taught you to cheat at cards. These days, he doesn't always remember your name.
- N: And every Saturday, he asks the same question.
- GRANDPA: Is Rosie coming today?
- N: Grandma Rosie died two years ago.
- N: The first time, you tell him the truth. And you watch him hear it for the very first time. He cries like it just happened. Because for him... it did.
- N: For many people with dementia, bad news doesn't stay learned. Every time they hear it, the grief can feel brand new. So some caregivers choose a gentle answer instead.
- N: The next Saturday, he asks again.
- GRANDPA: Is Rosie coming today?
- YOU: She's on her way, Grandpa.
- N: Pop. Twenty. He smiles all afternoon.

## ACT 5 — SPENDING YEARS
- N: So you make a choice. Every Saturday. One lie. One year.
- N: Twenty-one. He hums her favorite song. Twenty-five. He combs his hair for her. Thirty. Your knees start to ache on the stairs. Thirty-six. Your mom finds a gray hair on your hoodie... and it's yours.
- MOM: Eli. What is happening to you?
- N: You don't answer. Because the truth would sound like a lie.

## ACT 6 — THE TWIST
- N: Forty-one. And then one Saturday, Grandpa doesn't ask about Rosie. He looks at your face. Really looks.
- GRANDPA: You've got her curse.
- N: Wait. What?
- GRANDPA: Your grandmother. Every little lie... one more year. I married her at twenty. She looked forty-five.
- N: She told him he was a great dancer. He wasn't. She told your mom the dog went to live on a farm. And every night for fifty years, she told him she wasn't tired. Every lie she ever told... was for somebody else.
- GRANDPA: She spent her years on us, kiddo. Don't you spend yours on me.
- N: Then he asks you one last time.
- GRANDPA: So. Tell me the truth. Is Rosie coming?
- YOU: No, Grandpa. She's not.
- N: He nods. He squeezes your hand.
- GRANDPA: Good. Now go live. I'll be fine.
- N: It's the kindest lie you've ever heard.

## ACT 7 — RESOLUTION
- N: You're fifteen years old. Your body is forty-one. You can grow a full beard, but you still can't get a driver's license. And you've never had friends who trusted you more.
- N: You still visit Grandpa every Saturday. You just tell him the truth now. And somehow... it still makes him smile.

## ACT 8 — CLIFFHANGER + CTA
- N: Then, at dinner, your little sister knocks a glass off the table.
- MOM: Nora! Did you do that?
- NORA: Nope!
- N: Nothing happens. You breathe out. And then you see it. One gray hair. On a seven-year-old.
- N: So... would you spend a year of your life on a kind lie? Tell us in the comments. And subscribe. Because Nora is about to learn the rules.

### Fact check (goes in description)
- 1,000 US adults, mean 1.65 lies/24h; ~60% reported no lies; ~half of all lies told by ~5% of people —
  Serota, Levine & Boster (2010), "The Prevalence of Lying in America: Three Studies of
  Self-Reported Lies", Human Communication Research 36(1).
- 15 + 1.65 × 45.5 days ≈ 90 → "about six weeks". ✔
- Dementia: repeated bad news can be re-experienced as new grief; "therapeutic fibbing" /
  validation is debated in dementia care — Mental Health Foundation (2016), "What is truth?
  An inquiry about truth and lying in dementia care"; Alzheimer's Society guidance on
  communicating about a death.
- Age ledger: 15 →16 (homework) →17 (cookie) →18 (fine) →19 (good, thanks) →20 (Rosie #1)
  → weekly Saturdays… →41. ✔ consistent everywhere on screen.
