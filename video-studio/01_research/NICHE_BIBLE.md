## 2. Title formulas that work
- `What If You [had/could] [power] [multiplier]?` — "10x Faster", "500 IQ", "100x"
- `What If You [X] Every Time You [everyday action]?` — rule + cost (sorry→inch). Newest, hottest.
- `What If You Could See Everyone's [hidden number]?` — IQ, lie count, body count, regret
- `What If You Were The Last [X] on Earth?`
- Always: second person "You", ≤ 60 chars, a number when possible, a question mark.

## 3. Thumbnail formula (from PDF)
- 2–3 words MAX, huge rounded heavy font, white + one accent (yellow/red), thick dark outline.
  ("ONLY 2 LEFT", "TOO EASY?", "CATCH ME.", "LOOP BROKE.", "∞ MONEY?")
- Black round-head character, big white eyes, ONE strong emotion (shock, smug, panic).
- Number badges/tags with arrows ("$150" vs "$8,742,619,300", "160 IQ" vs "70 IQ").
- Before/after split ("DAY 1 | DAY 8"). High saturation, rim light, depth-of-field background.
- Thumbnail text never repeats the title — it adds tension.

## 4. Visual language
- Characters: black silhouette, round head, 2 white oval eyes (pupils move), no mouth
  except on emotion beats; thin limbs; ONE colored garment = identity (red hoodie, mint hoodie, yellow coat).
  Supporting characters keep the same rig, different hair silhouette/clothes color.
- Worlds: flat-vector 2.5D sets with parallax layers, soft gradients, volumetric light
  (window god-rays, street lamps), gentle grain/vignette. Palettes per act
  (warm day → cold crisis → warm resolution). refB = neon purple/magenta night.
- Data-viz interludes every 30–60s: dark navy (#0B1530) panels, thin cyan/white lines,
  bar charts, counters rolling, bell curves, timelines, maps — the "science beat".
- Big face close-ups (head fills frame) for emotion reactions — refB uses these a lot.
- On-screen text: small rounded badges with a fact ("≈ 10 kids per woman"), date/year
  tags top-right ("YEAR 20", "1790"), counters, chalkboard writing.
- refC adds single-word kinetic captions bottom-center (white, heavy, outlined) —
  optional; refA/B don't use captions.

## 5. Editing grammar (measured)
- Cut detection: refB 107 cuts / 409s = **3.8s avg shot**; refC 3.8s; refA 17s (long
  camera moves inside one set instead of cuts — camera pans/push-ins ARE the cut).
- Montage beats: 1.5–2.5s shots. Story beats: 3.5–6s. Never a static frame >2s: always
  a slow push (1.00→1.06 scale) or parallax drift.
- Transitions: 80% hard cuts on the VO beat; zoom-through (push into door/screen/eye),
  split-screen wipe for +/- contrasts, whip pans for montages, match cuts (shape→shape),
  fade-to-black ONLY for the darkest beat and the cliffhanger.
- Chapter feel without title cards: door push-ins, date tags.

## 6. Sound design (measured + AI watch)
- Final loudness: refs at **-14 to -16 LUFS integrated**, LRA ~5 LU. Target −14 LUFS, TP −1 dBTP.
- Music changes mood every 60–90s (curious lo-fi → tense drone → warm keys → driving
  pulse → triumphant → ominous cliffhanger). Music ducks ~10–12 dB under VO.
- Music DROPS OUT completely for 1–2s before the biggest line (silence = emphasis).
- SFX on almost every visual action: whoosh on moves/transitions, pop/blip on text
  badges, tick on counters, ding/chime on UI, thud/impact on reveals, risers before
  reveals, stamp thud, phone buzz, room tone, crowd murmur, footsteps.
- Character voices: short lines only, narrator carries 90%.

## 7. Voiceover
- Male, ~30s, warm, neutral American, slightly wry. 140–160 WPM.
- 100% second person present tense: "You wake up. Your knee cracks."
- Short sentences. Fragments. Pauses of 0.4–1.0s before punchlines and stats.
- Fact lines delivered plainly, then a human consequence line right after.

## 8. Story structure (both top videos follow it)
1. **0:00–0:15 Cold open in the middle of the mechanic** — show the power/curse
   happening before explaining it (phone battery 31 days / sorry→pop→taller).
2. **0:15–0:35 The premise line + one real stat** ("Most people score ~100… you? 500.")
3. **Act 1 "It's amazing"** — fun montage, small wins, comedy.
4. **Act 1b "The catch"** — the rule bites. Real science/statistic beat.
5. **Act 2 escalation** — costs rise, relationships strain, a hard number/deadline appears
   (doctor draws a line on the chart: 8 ft).
6. **Act 3 the emotional anchor** — mom / sister / best friend. A choice that costs
   the hero something real.
7. **Climax** — the hero spends the cost on purpose for someone else.
8. **Warm resolution** — callback to the cold open.
9. **Final 15s** — question to the comments + twist/cliffhanger for Part 2.
- Open loops every ~45s ("And that's when it starts going wrong.").
- Real, sourced facts in description ("Is any of this real?") → trust + comments.

## 9. What makes it NOT slop (our quality bar)
- Every shot has motion + a sound.
- Characters ACT (blink, look, react) — not sliding cutouts.
- Consistent world/palette per act, rim light, depth.
- Real facts, checked, in the description.
- An emotional story, not a list of consequences.
