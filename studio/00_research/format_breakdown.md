## What the format is
A **taxonomy video**: take one real-world coding/classification system people see every day but
never understood, show *every* member as a labelled tile, then explain them one by one.

## Title patterns
1. `Every <Thing> (Color) Explained` — dominant (10 of 11 titled videos).
2. `How <System> Actually Works` — the two outliers (1.9M, 14K) use this; "Actually" implies a hidden truth.
3. Optional `(Part 2)` for series.
- Short: 4–7 words. No numbers, no emojis, no clickbait punctuation.

## Thumbnail grammar
- **Grid of N labelled tiles** (6 to 32). The count itself is the hook: "I recognise 3 of these… what are the rest?"
- Each tile: one isolated object, centred, consistent scale and angle, short ALL-CAPS or small-caps label under it.
- Background: plain white/light grey tiles, or dark (`#111`) tiles for "serious" topics (lights, shells, monitors).
- Objects are **colour-coded variants of one shape** (same hydrant/helmet/bin/shell in 9 colours) → instant pattern recognition.
- **Curiosity anchor**: one tile is broken out of the pattern and marked in red —
  `?` box ("WHERE IS THIS SHIP?", "General of the Armies"), a red stamp ("GIVEN AWAY"), or an odd one out (tiger-stripe bin).
  Both of the top two non-Unclassified thumbnails use it.
- Style variants seen: photo-real cutouts (hydrants, buoys, medals), flat vector (bins, hard hats, lights, bullets),
  hand-drawn sketch (ranks — the 1.9M video).
- No faces, no big text overlays, no arrows except the red highlight.

## What performs (early signal, small sample)
- Military / weapons and "everyday object you walk past" topics lead (ranks 1.9M, bullet tips 395K, hydrants 195K).
- Length does not predict views: 5:43 → 195K, 14:32 → 70K. Topic + thumbnail does.
- Hand-drawn and photo-real grids both work; clarity of the grid matters more than the style.

## Inferred video structure (to verify against real videos)
1. Cold open: the full grid on screen, "You've seen these… but do you know what they mean?" (≤15 s).
2. Optional context: who invented the system / which standard (NFPA, ANSI, NATO…).
3. Item by item, in grid order: zoom into tile → name → meaning → one surprising fact → back to grid with the tile ticked.
4. The red "?" item is held to the end (retention hook) and paid off last.
5. Short outro, no long CTA.

## Open questions (need the real videos)
- Words per minute, sentence length, VO tone and gender.
- Music bed: genre, level, does it change per section?
- SFX vocabulary: whooshes, clicks, pops, typewriter, camera shutter?
- Camera: zoom-into-grid vs. cut-to-full-screen per item; transition style and duration.
- Intro: branded sting or straight in? How long before the first item?
- How often does the visual change (seconds per shot)?
- Mid-roll retention tricks (re-showing the grid, progress counter, "the last one is the strangest").
