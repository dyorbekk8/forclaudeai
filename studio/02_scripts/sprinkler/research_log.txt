Conflicts flagged:
  - Blue: NFSA mentions 250°F (class floor) vs 286°F (common rating).
  - Purple vs mauve naming.
  - Black is a range, not 500°F only.
  - Frame colours (red frame = extra high) — only one source for red/green/orange frames.
  - Glass-bulb invention date (1890 disc vs 1920 bulb vs 1929 pamphlet) — unresolved.
  - Parmelee "first": Pratt 1872 patent exists.
  - MGM death toll 84/85/87.
  - Dumfries suit amount $690K vs $700K; plaintiff insurer vs hotel.
  - Thayer bride amount $77K vs $87.6K.
  - "90% of fires one head" (industry) vs NFPA 77% (2015–2019) / 79% (2010–2014). Use NFPA.

Cold-open candidates (ranked):
  1. MGM Grand, 21 Nov 1980 — 85 dead; sprinklers left out to save ~$192,000 on a $106M resort; NFPA: "a one- or two-sprinkler fire".
  2. Parmelee, 11 Aug 1874 — the first practical automatic sprinkler head was patented by a piano maker to protect pianos / cut insurance, not to save people.
  3. Dumfries Holiday Inn, 20 Oct 2017 — a cadet's uniform on one head flooded 10 rooms + kitchen; ~$700K suit. (Fits "only one head opens" myth-bust; lower stakes.)
