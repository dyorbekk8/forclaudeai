# Finding your first 20 leads

Goal: 20 real, qualified store owners with a name, an email (or a way to
reach them), and a one-line reason ShopMate fits them — not 20 random
Instagram accounts.

## Where to look

1. **Instagram/TikTok Shop hashtag + explore search.** Search hashtags like
   `#smallbusinessowner`, `#shopifystore`, `#onlineboutique`,
   `#handmadebusiness` combined with a niche (`#skincarebrand`,
   `#streetwearbrand`). Open accounts with 1,000-20,000 followers, check
   their bio for a website/Linktree, and check recent posts' comments for
   unanswered customer questions — that's your qualifying signal (see
   `icp.md`).

2. **Shopify's public storefronts.** Many Shopify stores list "Powered by
   Shopify" in their footer — searching Google for niche terms +
   `site:myshopify.com` or browsing directories like Shopify's own app
   store reviews (store owners often link their site in reviews) surfaces
   real stores fast.

3. **Etsy/Depop/Poshmark sellers going independent.** Sellers who mention
   "also on Instagram @..." or "check out my own site" in their shop
   descriptions are often outgrowing marketplace fees and open to owning
   more of the customer relationship — a good pitch angle for a
   direct-to-customer bot.

4. **Facebook Groups for e-commerce/dropshipping/Shopify owners.** Search
   Facebook groups (many are public or easy to join) where store owners
   discuss their businesses — profile links in posts/comments lead
   straight to real, currently-active stores.

5. **LinkedIn — "Founder" / "Owner" + industry.** For higher-ticket niches
   (home goods, B2C subscription boxes), LinkedIn search for
   "Founder" + "skincare" or similar surfaces named decision-makers
   directly, often with a company website in their profile.

## Qualifying before you add someone to the list

For each candidate, spend under 2 minutes checking:
- [ ] Do they sell a repeatable catalog of products? (not one-off custom work)
- [ ] Follower/subscriber count roughly 1,000-20,000?
- [ ] Any visible sign they answer customers manually or slowly?
- [ ] Can you find a real email or a fast way to reach them (site contact
      form, "for business inquiries" email in bio)?

If yes to all four, add them to `leads_template.csv`. Suggested `status`
values as you work the list: `new` → `emailed_1` → `emailed_2` →
`emailed_3` → `replied` → `demo_sent` → `won` / `lost`.

## Finding an email when only a DM/contact form is visible

- Check the website footer/contact/about page — many list a support email
- Try `hello@<domain>`, `support@<domain>`, `info@<domain>` — common
  patterns for small stores (verify deliverability before sending in bulk,
  see below)
- If truly nothing, a DM/contact-form-based first touch is fine — just
  keep it short and mention you'll follow up by email if they share one

## Before sending anything

Read `agency-growth-mentor`'s guidance (available as a skill in this
workspace) on deliverability: warm up any new sending domain gradually,
verify each email before a real send if you're compiling a list quickly,
and start with volume low enough that a bad batch doesn't burn your
domain's reputation. Getting 20 *real, qualified* leads and sending 20
careful, personalized-enough emails beats blasting 200 unqualified ones.
